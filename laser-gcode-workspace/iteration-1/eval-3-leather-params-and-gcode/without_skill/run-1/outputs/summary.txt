SUMMARY - Leather laser cutting G-code
======================================

Output file : circle_40mm_leather.gcode
Format      : GRBL (laser mode, $32=1 or 2)

MATERIAL
  2mm veg-tanned leather (植鞣皮革)

MACHINE
  20W diode laser (455nm blue)

TARGET SHAPE
  Circle, diameter 40 mm
  Approximated by a 24-segment polygon (linear G1 moves)
  Center at X50 Y50 mm, radius 20 mm
  First vertex at (70, 50) - angle 0 deg

CUTTING PARAMETERS (chosen)
  Power (S)   : 1000  (100% on the 0-1000 GRBL scale)
  Feed rate   : 150 mm/min
  Passes      : 4  (path repeated 4 times)
  Laser mode  : M3 constant power during G1 moves
  Travel      : G0 rapid

WHY THESE VALUES
  - 20W diode lasers are weakly absorbed by leather vs CO2; full power
    and slow feed are needed to cut through 2mm.
  - 150 mm/min is a conservative cut speed for 2mm leather on a 20W
    diode; slower = cleaner cut but more scorch.
  - 4 passes help ensure a through-cut; leather thickness/finish vary,
    so test on a scrap piece first.

SAFETY NOTES (also in the .gcode header)
  1. Wear 455nm-rated laser safety goggles at all times - the beam is
     near-visible and hazardous to eyes.
  2. Leather can char and flame - keep a fire extinguisher nearby and
     never leave the machine unattended.
  3. Ensure ventilation / exhaust extraction for smoke and fumes.
  4. Confirm GRBL laser mode is enabled: send $32=1 (or 2) before run.
  5. Watch the first pass; if edges scorch too much, reduce power to
     S850 or raise feed to 200 mm/min.
  6. Verify workpiece fits within machine travel and the beam is
     focused on the leather surface.
  7. The G-code starts with M4 S0 (laser off) and ends with M5 / M2.

TUNING TIPS
  - If it does not cut through: lower feed to 100 mm/min, or add a 5th
    pass, or raise power (already at max).
  - If edges are badly burnt: raise feed to 200 mm/min, drop power to
    S850, or reduce passes to 3.
  - Air assist is strongly recommended - it reduces charring and helps
    clear debris from the kerf.
